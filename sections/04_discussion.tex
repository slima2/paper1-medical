\section*{Discussion}

Computational pathology has converged on a single output for
mutation prediction: a probability per gene.
The implicit assumption is that this probability is comparable
across genes and that improvement on the cohort-mean metric is
the figure of merit.
Our results challenge both assumptions.
The same cohort, same encoder, same MIL backbone yields
qualitatively different gene-level behaviour: encoder
saturation on TP53/EGFR (where additional pattern information
adds nothing), inter-pattern co-occurrence on KRAS (where only
a non-additive aggregator captures the IMA proxy), and an
unusable pattern channel on STK11/KEAP1/RBM10---out-of-ontology
for the first two, whose signal lives in stromal or biochemical
substrates the 6-pattern ontology does not encode, but merely
unestimable for RBM10, whose pattern-side atoms are demonstrably
non-empty at $n_+\!=\!37$.
The Conclusive/Inconclusive flag we report alongside each AUROC
is therefore not a cosmetic addition---it is a statement that
the cohort-level metric does \emph{not} translate uniformly into
per-slide actionability, and that downstream clinical use must
be gene-conditional.

Reading the results gene-conditionally also changes what counts as
the contribution.
The pattern-informed channel does not win the cohort-mean
benchmark---across the upper tier of
\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec} the fusion arms and the
embedding-only baseline sit within about $0.02$ AUROC of one another
on every gene, a gap well inside the corresponding per-fold standard
deviation, which runs from $0.023$ to $0.082$ across that tier
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}), and therefore not a cohort-mean
improvement we would defend.
This is precisely why the regime is read from the \emph{direction} of the
channel contrasts rather than from their magnitude (Methods): the sign of an
interaction is stable exactly where a $0.02$ AUROC gap is within-fold noise.
What pattern-informed MIL does win is the \emph{argument} with the
pathologist.
A model that says ``P(KRAS) = 0.965 because the
lepidic$\,\times\,$solid Choquet interaction is $+0.032$ on the
top-200 attended tiles'' (\cref{fig:kras_lepidic_solid_proxy})
invites a textbook reply about the
invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma proxy.
A model that instead reports $P(\text{KEAP1}) = 0.610$ with a
negligible Choquet interaction and a SHAP/ablation split that places
almost all of the slide-level signal in the embedding channel and
essentially none in the pattern channel
(\cref{tab:pid_regimes}, with the exact figures in Results) is transparent
about what the slide cannot answer, and points the next investigation
toward NRF2 immunostaining or RNA-seq rather than another loss
function.
This is the central clinical claim of the paper: \emph{when the
predictor is wrong, the explanation is most valuable}, and only a
pattern-informed pipeline gives the explanation a vocabulary the
pathologist can act on.\cite{rudin2019stop,holzinger2019causability}

Read through the translation lens introduced above, our results
belong to a well-established family of medical machine-learning
tasks in which one modality is mapped onto a target it does not
directly measure, with a
faithfulness that is bounded by how much of the target signal the
source physically encodes.
Placing our LUAD map beside these tasks clarifies both what is and
what is not surprising about the per-gene ceiling
(\cref{fig:fidelity_spectrum}).
The family runs from virtual staining, the literal image-to-image
case in which tissue autofluorescence is translated into stained
histology at pathologist-level fidelity precisely because the source
physically contains the target\cite{rivenson2019virtualstaining},
through ECG-to-function mapping, where an electrical modality
faithfully encodes a structural one ($\mathrm{EF}\!\leq\!35\%$,
$\auroc=0.93$)\cite{attia2019ecg}, and retinal-to-systemic mapping,
which recovers cardiovascular risk factors from fundus photographs
and so surfaces signal not previously thought to be morphologically
encoded\cite{poplin2018retinal}, to pathomics-to-genomics on the
very H\&E substrate we use\cite{kather2019msi,kather2020pancancer}.
Radiogenomics is the most instructive of them, because it already
exhibits the phenomenon we formalise: on one TCIA cohort ($n\!=\!259$)
IDH and 1p/19q are recovered at $\auroc=0.91$ and $0.88$ while
MGMT-promoter methylation trails at
$0.81$\cite{chang2018radiogenomics}. That per-marker split---same
cohort, same architecture, three different outcomes---is of the same
kind as our per-gene regimes. The MGMT figure itself does not hold up,
and the manner of its failure matters. It is a five-fold
cross-validated mean on a single dataset with no external cohort, and
what it fails to survive is not only the move between
cohorts\cite{kim2022mgmtvalidation}: it does not reproduce
\emph{within} one either. On five patient-disjoint folds of the larger,
balanced BraTS-2021 cohort---the same internal design---the
unconstrained encoder sits at $0.562$ (\cref{tab:glioma_domainb}), and
the BraTS-2021 challenge was itself won at only
$\auroc\!\approx\!0.62$ on its own validation
split\cite{baid2021brats}. Our two cross-domain tests therefore measure
two different things, and we keep them apart: CPTAC is a transfer
failure, where the one well-powered gene is Conclusive on TCGA and
collapses under distribution shift (\cref{tab:cptac_transfer}); MGMT is
an absent signal, already visible without leaving the cohort at all.

Against this backdrop our LUAD map sits at the
\emph{low-faithfulness, low-data} corner
(\cref{fig:fidelity_spectrum}; per-gene
$\auroc\in[0.6,0.72]$ in \cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec};
positive sets down to $n_+\!=\!37$ for RBM10).
The three-regime taxonomy is the diagnostic that explains
\emph{why} a given gene lands there---encoder saturation, a missing
non-additive grammar, or genuine out-of-ontology
untranslatability---and therefore whether the remedy is a better
translator (architecture), a richer source vocabulary
(micro-environment channels) or a different source modality
altogether (RNA-seq, radiology).
The recurring lesson across all of these tasks is the one our
Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict operationalises: the value of a
cross-domain map is set by the physics of the source, not by the
sophistication of the estimator, and transparent per-target
reporting of that ceiling is what turns the map into a usable
clinical tool.

What lifts this from an empirical grouping to a portable claim is
that the regimes are not a grouping at all.
A taxonomy that merely groups genes by their AUROC is a description
of one cohort and travels no further than that cohort.
The three regimes are instead a decomposition: the pattern channel
and the embedding channel are two sources of information about the
same target, and \cref{eq:pid} states the only four ways that
information can be distributed between them
(\cref{tab:pid_regimes}).\cite{williams2010pid,bertschinger2014unique}
Once stated this way, the taxonomy makes commitments that can be
checked, and the check reported in Results---that the non-additive
aggregator wins on exactly the genes with a recoverable synergistic
atom and loses elsewhere---is the kind of evidence a leaderboard
comparison cannot produce.
The objection this has to answer is that the regimes only restate what
is already known: that KEAP1 acts through a biochemical axis, that
STK11 acts through the immune micro-environment. Recovering those is
\emph{calibration}---an instrument earns trust by returning the known
answer where the answer is known---and we present it as such. The
predictive content lies where prior knowledge does not reach, and two
instances are on the record here. The additive sub-form (ii-a) was an
empty cell of \cref{eq:pid} in LUAD, predicted by the decomposition
before anything occupied it, and it is populated by 1p/19q in a
different modality (\cref{tab:glioma_domainb}). And KEAP1 and RBM10,
separated by $0.05$ AUROC (\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}) and
indistinguishable on any leaderboard, carry opposite atom structures and
therefore opposite next experiments. What the framework predicts is not
the mutation but which remedy will move it.

The specific bridge we draw is between the $2$-additive Choquet
interaction index and the synergistic atom.
These are objects of different kinds: $I_{jk}$ is a learnable,
differentiable, per-slide parameter of an aggregation operator,
whereas $\Isyn$ is a property of a joint distribution that is
notoriously hard to estimate in high dimension.
The correspondence we establish is one of sign and support---a
supra-additive fuzzy measure on a pattern pair is the aggregator's
way of paying for joint activation, which is what a positive
synergistic atom licenses---and it is what makes the atom
inspectable at the bedside rather than only in a contingency table.
We do not claim that $I_{jk}$ is a consistent estimator of
$\Isyn$; establishing the conditions under which it is, or bounding
the gap, is the natural theoretical continuation of this work.

The decomposition itself is not new, and it is worth being precise
about what is.
\Cref{eq:pid} has been used for a decade to ask how a neuron integrates
its inputs, how baroreflex and respiratory pathways jointly drive heart
rate variability,\cite{krohova2019hrv} and which gene triplets carry
regulatory information a pairwise measure would
miss.\cite{chan2017pidc}
In the modality of our Domain~B it has been used to rank MRI contrast
pairs and retain only the most informative when training under a compute
budget.\cite{chopra2026pidmri}
It was brought to multimodal learning with estimators that scale to
high-dimensional sources, and applied there to computational pathology:
in TCGA lower-grade glioma every atom was near zero except the one
unique to genomics, which the authors read as evidence that histology
was not the modality carrying the answer.\cite{liang2023quantifying}
That is the same shape of conclusion as our regime~(iii), and we take it
as corroboration rather than as competition, because two things differ.
The first is the axis. Every application above decomposes across
\emph{different} sources---pathology against genomics, pressure against
respiration, gene against gene, contrast against
contrast---whereas the two channels here are two
readings of one slide, which is what allows the question to be whether a
clinically legible vocabulary adds anything to an opaque encoder.
The second is the purpose. Those studies use the decomposition to choose
a fusion architecture; the recent analysis closest to ours uses a
related bound to ask whether a translated representation adds
information to the one it was derived from.\cite{saisan2026ceilings}
We use it to adjudicate whether the target is reachable from the
modality at all, and to attach a distinct remedy to each verdict.

This literature also supplies the strongest reason for the choice made
in Methods not to quote numerical atoms. When the four \pid{} statistics
were estimated directly across seven modality pairs in four cancer
cohorts, they agreed with downstream model performance in only one, and
the authors concluded that the estimators are not yet accurate enough to
guide a fusion decision.\cite{zhang2025evaluatingpid}
Reading the atoms as directional signatures from trained contrasts is
not a concession to intractability, then, but the more defensible
instrument at these sample sizes: a sign that survives a paired
bootstrap is a weaker claim than a point estimate, and, on the present
evidence, a considerably more reliable one.

The practical consequence is a change in how the field should
decide what to build next.
Under a cohort-mean metric, the question ``what should we add?''
has one answer for all genes and it is usually more parameters or
more slides.
Under \cref{eq:pid} the question is atom-conditional: a redundant
atom means no aggregator change will help and the encoder is the
binding constraint; a synergistic atom means the aggregator's
hypothesis space is the binding constraint; an empty pattern-side
atom means the \emph{source vocabulary} is the binding constraint
and only a new channel will move it; and a pattern-side atom that is
non-empty but unestimable means the \emph{cohort} is the binding
constraint, so more positives of the same kind are exactly what is
needed. The last two matter most in practice because they are
indistinguishable on a leaderboard and call for opposite investments:
KEAP1 will not yield to more slides at any number, RBM10 plausibly will.

The translation frame does more than organise the results; it
suggests a different stance on interpretability itself.
Mainstream explainable-AI methods for computational pathology are
\emph{post-hoc and attributive}: a saliency map, an attention
overlay or a SHAP value indicates \emph{where} in the slide the
model attended, but not in a vocabulary the pathologist can contest
line by line.\cite{rudin2019stop,jain2019attention}
Reading the pipeline as a translator reframes explanation as the
act of \emph{exhibiting the translation}: the six-level stack (C2)
is a bilingual dictionary that must express every prediction in the
source language of growth patterns (L2), expose the non-additive
\emph{grammar} that couples them (L4 Choquet--Shapley interactions),
and hand the result to a target-domain lexicon (the NCIt$+$MONDO
knowledge graph, C4) that forbids invented genotype terms.
Interpretability becomes contrastive and bilingual rather than a
heat-coloured monologue.

The sharper consequence is that \emph{untranslatability is promoted
to a first-class, actionable result}---provided it is asserted at the
level where it was established.
This is the qualification we most want to press, because the
temptation the framework creates is precisely to skip it.
The Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict thresholds a mean \auroc{}, a
property of the cohort and not of any slide, so reading a low cohort
mean as ``this mutation cannot be seen in this tissue'' is a level
error---and a consequential one, because it discards exactly the cases
where the morphology \emph{does} speak.
KRAS is our counterexample and it is unambiguous
(\cref{fig:kras_lepidic_solid_proxy}): a gene that is Inconclusive as
a population ($\auroc = 0.609$, \cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec})
yet carries confident,
tissue-localised per-slide calls wherever the IMA-associated
lepidic$\times$solid morphology is present.
Nothing about those slides is untranslatable.
What is true is that the subpopulation in which the translation holds
is a minority of the cohort, so the average over case pairs is
mediocre---a statement about population heterogeneity, not about the
expressive capacity of the source modality.

Untranslatability proper is therefore a narrower and stronger claim:
that the source-side atoms of \cref{eq:pid} are empty at the level
being discussed.
When a gene meets it, the principled output is not a low probability
presented as if it were a confident call, but the statement that the
mutation cannot be said in morphological words---and, via the
per-slide channel ablation (L5) and the SHAP embedding/pattern split
(L3), \emph{why}: the signal is in the immune micro-environment
(STK11) or in a biochemical axis with no H\&E correlate (KEAP1).
A translation that returns ``this sentence has no equivalent in the
target language, and here is the missing vocabulary'' is more useful
to the next experiment---NRF2 immunostaining, TIL quantification,
RNA-seq---than a fluent but ungrounded paraphrase, and it is the
decomposition that makes the statement specific enough to act on:
the four states an Inconclusive verdict can hide
(\cref{tab:levels_of_analysis}) share a mediocre AUROC while calling for
different experiments.
The operational rule that follows is deliberately restrictive. An
Inconclusive gene licenses no diagnostic use by the reporting
pathologist---not a hedged call, not a ranked shortlist---until the
deficit the decomposition names has been repaired. What the regime adds
is \emph{which} repair counts: an empty pattern-side atom is not repaired
by more slides at any number, only by a new source channel; an
unestimable one is repaired by more positives of the same kind; a diluted
subpopulation is not repaired at the cohort level at all, but by moving
the unit of deployment to the slide, where KRAS is already
well-calibrated; and a heterogeneous target is repaired by refining the
label rather than the model. Reporting the verdict without the regime
would leave the clinician with a prohibition and no route out of it.
We therefore position the Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict as a
population-level translatability flag rather than as a per-case
confidence threshold or as a verdict on the modality, and we argue
that reporting untranslatability explicitly---with its level
attached---is a distinct and underused mode of interpretability for
cross-domain medical prediction.

A framework that has only ever been run on the cohort it was designed
around is indistinguishable from a description of that cohort.
Because the decomposition of \cref{eq:pid} needs only its two
ingredients---an expert controlled vocabulary and an unconstrained learned
encoder of the same modality---both available far from pathology, we
instantiate it on two further public cohorts
(\cref{tab:crossdomain_protocol}), specified so that the framework can be
falsified independently of the LUAD results.

The level structure of \cref{tab:levels_of_analysis} transfers with
the framework. In every domain the source channels are defined on a
single \emph{unit}---a slide in LUAD, a scan in glioma, a record in
ECG---but the \pid{} atoms of \cref{eq:pid} and the
Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict of \cref{eq:conclusive_verdict} are
expectations over the joint distribution of those channels and the
label, and are therefore \emph{population}-level in all three cases;
probability and the L1--L6 interpretability instruments remain
per-unit (\cref{tab:levels_crossdomain}). Two consequences follow.
First, the population-level synergistic atom $\mathcal{S}$ has a
per-unit counterpart in the Choquet interaction index $I_{jk}$, so
the non-additive sub-form of regime~(ii) is falsifiable at both levels
in each domain---between
growth patterns on a slide, between VASARI features on a scan, and
between SCP-ECG statements on a record. Second, an Inconclusive
population verdict is compatible with the same states of the
world in every domain (information absent at every level; present in a
morphologically or radiologically defined subpopulation and diluted in
the average; or present but unestimable at the available positive-set
size), so the per-stratum readout we use to adjudicate KRAS here is the
same instrument used to weigh strict MGMT untranslatability against a
diluted VASARI stratum in domain~B. The only structural difference is
that a slide and a scan are \emph{bags} aggregated by MIL, whereas an
ECG record is a \emph{single instance}: its sub-unit level collapses
and synergy is read between vocabulary statements directly.

The first instantiation, glioma MRI $\rightarrow$ genotype, is the
closest structural analogue: an image of a tumour mapped
onto its molecular profile, on open data, with an expert vocabulary
already in place.
VASARI is a consensus-defined controlled terminology of roughly
thirty semantic MR features---enhancement quality, necrosis, margin
definition, oedema, ependymal contact, grey-level
heterogeneity---published with per-subject scores and reported
inter-observer agreement for the TCGA glioma
collections.\cite{gutman2013vasari,vasari_tcia}
The transfer of our pipeline is one-to-one: a VASARI-feature
classifier trained on the TCIA-annotated seed ($n=178$ LGG and $n=75$
GBM carry expert scores, of which $63$ LGG also carry the co-registered
preoperative MRI and molecular call the protocol needs) and propagated
to the $\sim$1{,}251 BraTS 2021
subjects\cite{baid2021brats} occupies the position that
\fuzzyarc{}\,V2 trained on Zenodo-ANORAK and propagated to TCGA-LUAD
occupies here in the LUAD two-channel architecture
(\cref{fig:two_channels}), with the same 2-additive Choquet aggregator
over the feature vector (\cref{fig:pipeline_glioma}).

What makes this domain worth the effort is the target, not the
method.
MGMT promoter methylation has resisted prediction from preoperative
MRI for a decade. Beyond the BraTS 2021 challenge result already
noted, CNN, ViT and Swin variants evaluated externally collapse to
chance
($\auroc\!\approx\!0.50$--$0.56$),\cite{baid2021brats,saeed2023mgmt} a systematic
re-evaluation concluded that the algorithms developed to date are
``not suitable for clinical application'',\cite{robinet2023mgmt} and
an external validation across $985$ patients concluded that the
methylation status ``may not be predictable'' from conventional
structural MRI even with deep
learning.\cite{kim2022mgmtvalidation}
That literature is, in our terms, a decade of accumulating evidence
for a regime-(iii) verdict---reported as a community-wide defeat
because no framework existed in which it could be reported as a
finding.
Instantiated, the prediction is specific and now largely tested: IDH sits
in regime~(i) with the VASARI channel adding nothing on top of the encoder;
1p/19q sits in regime~(ii), recovered by fusion; and MGMT sits in
regime~(iii).
The MGMT test is the strong one, because it asserts the strict form of
untranslatability and has to exclude the two weaker states that produce the
same stalled AUROC: a data floor, which the $n\!=\!577$ balanced BraTS
cohort (${\sim}52\%$ methylated) excludes outright, and a subpopulation
effect of the kind KRAS exhibits here.
Scored at full power every condition sits at chance
(\cref{tab:glioma_domainb}: $\mathrm{B2}\!=\!0.56$, $\mathrm{B3}\!=\!0.53$,
$\mathrm{PI}\!=\!0.53$, $\mathrm{FC}\!=\!0.55$) and the verdict is
Inconclusive.
Because the VASARI channel here is \emph{propagated} from the $63$-subject
seed rather than expert-scored (held-out feature $\auroc\!\lesssim\!0.6$), we
rest the claim on the unconstrained encoder remaining at chance, not on the
strict empty-atom versus data-floor separation that real VASARI licenses for
KEAP1/RBM10 in LUAD.
If that verdict holds, the remedy is a different source
modality---perfusion or diffusion sequences, or methylation-array
fusion---rather than a further architecture on structural MRI.

A first, deliberately small instantiation is runnable without any of the
propagation machinery: the ${\sim}63$ TCGA-LGG subjects that carry real
neuroradiologist VASARI scores, co-registered preoperative MRI from
BraTS-TCGA-LGG (\cref{fig:glioma_example}) and a matched molecular call. Running the identical
four-condition protocol five-fold patient-disjoint on this cohort
(\cref{tab:glioma_domainb}) reproduces the predicted regime structure at the
level the sample size supports. IDH is Conclusive and encoder-saturated
(regime~(i)): the encoder alone reaches $\auroc\!=\!0.86$ and the fusion arms
add nothing beyond a fold's standard deviation, matching the radiogenomic
expectation. 1p/19q is Conclusive and sits in regime~(ii) in its additive
sub-form (ii-a): neither channel alone is informative
($\mathrm{B2}\!=\!0.60$, $\mathrm{B3}\!=\!0.50$) while their concat fusion
recovers it ($\mathrm{PI}\!=\!0.74$, $+0.14$ over the encoder;
\cref{tab:glioma_domainb}), the
signature of complementary unique atoms rather than of a synergistic one.
A patient-sex control,
absent from the VASARI vocabulary, is correctly Inconclusive with the VASARI
channel below chance, exercising the guard against spurious certification.
One limit is genuinely deferred: the \emph{non-additive} sub-form (ii-b) of
the 1p/19q complementarity---the $\mathrm{FC}\!>\!\mathrm{PI}$ signature that
KRAS shows in LUAD---is not resolved at $n\!\sim\!63$, where the learned Choquet pair
interactions shrink to $\lvert I_{jk}\rvert\!\lesssim\!0.005$, so the
interaction read is deferred rather than asserted, and the expectation that
the non-additive aggregator would win on 1p/19q was our modelling
hypothesis, not an established radiogenomic result.

That 1p/19q landed in the additive rather than the synergistic sub-form is
worth stating plainly, because it is the sharpest evidence available here that
the taxonomy is derived rather than descriptive. \Cref{eq:pid} has always had
four atoms, but the original three-regime mapping invoked $\Iuni{\mathbf{P}}$
only in its \emph{absence}---regime~(iii) requires
$\Iuni{\mathbf{P}}\!\approx\!\Isyn\!\approx\!0$---for the contingent reason
that no TCGA-LUAD gene populated the cell in which the pattern channel carries
unique information that is nonetheless additively separable. Had the three
regimes been a relabelling of one cohort's AUROC table, a second domain would
have produced a case fitting none of them; because they are read off
\cref{eq:pid}, it produced one for which the decomposition already had a cell,
and the taxonomy absorbs it by naming a sub-form rather than by adding a
category. The transfer therefore did not weaken the regime-(ii) claim on
1p/19q---which stands on
$\mathrm{PI}\!>\!\max(\mathrm{B2},\mathrm{B3})$---but declined the stronger
sub-claim at a sample size that cannot support it.

The second instantiation, the electrocardiogram $\rightarrow$
physiological state, tests whether the taxonomy survives a change of
modality family. Whether it also scales past six targets is a question
the laboratory-target literature surveyed below poses and this run
does not settle.
PTB-XL supplies $21{,}799$ records annotated by cardiologists with
$71$ SCP-ECG statements organised into diagnostic, form and rhythm
categories with a proposed
hierarchy\cite{wagner2020ptbxl}---the direct counterpart of the
ANORAK six---and PTB-XL$+$ supplies the SNOMED-CT mapping with
upward propagation through the ontology tree, which is our
knowledge-graph layer already
built.\cite{strodthoff2023ptbxlplus}
Two properties make this domain more favourable than histology.
First, the source vocabulary is already \emph{graded}: each
diagnostic statement carries a likelihood score on a $0$--$100$ scale,
extracted from the cardiologist's report, so the fuzzy membership our
L6 layer must learn in LUAD is here supplied as data.
Second, the target set is an order of magnitude larger: a recent
foundation-model study reports $33$ of $108$ laboratory indicators
above $\auroc=0.65$, $59$ in a borderline band the authors describe
as information that ``may exist in the signal but is difficult to
extract'', and $16$ below $\auroc=0.55$\cite{xiao2025anyecglab}---an
empirical three-way partition published without a framework to
interpret it.
The sharpest single case is the contrast reported by
AnyECG-Lab\cite{xiao2025anyecglab} (not run here) between NT-proBNP
($\auroc\!\approx\!0.78$) and troponin~I ($\auroc<0.55$): two cardiac markers,
one with a projection onto the electrical signal and one a purely
biochemical state, mirroring our EGFR-versus-KEAP1 contrast
in a different organ system.
Structural heart disease labels from
EchoNext\cite{hughes2026echonext} and the paired
ECG/chest-radiograph/laboratory records of
MIMIC-IV-ECG\cite{gow2023mimicivecg} extend the same protocol to a
multimodal setting in which the missingness correction of
\citet{choi2025icym2i} becomes mandatory rather than optional.

Between them the two domains buy complementary evidence: the glioma
instantiation tests the framework against the hardest available negative
case (MGMT), while the electrocardiogram instantiation tests whether the
three regimes survive a change of modality family and whether a natively
graded source vocabulary strengthens or weakens the fuzzy explanation
layer. Whether they remain distinguishable at $10^2$ targets rather than
$6$ is the further question the laboratory targets pose, and it is not
one this paper answers. Neither requires new data collection, proprietary access
or a foundation-scale cohort; both are reachable from public archives, which
is the point.

The electrocardiogram domain, however, need not remain purely
pre-registered. Two of the ${\sim}10^{2}$ targets it affords are available
immediately: patient sex and age are recorded
for every PTB-XL case and are not derivable from the SCP-ECG statements
(which are diagnostic, form and rhythm terms), so they furnish a clean,
leakage-free pair on which the framework can be run now, with the same
two channels: the $71$ graded SCP-ECG statements as the source
vocabulary $\mathbf{P}$ and a compact 1D convolutional encoder of the raw
twelve-lead signal as $\mathbf{E}$ (\cref{fig:ecg_ptbxl}). This is the
electrocardiographic
counterpart of the retinal$\rightarrow$systemic mapping we cite---
recovering a physiological state from a modality not designed to report
it\cite{poplin2018retinal,attia2019ecg}.

Running the identical four-condition protocol used for LUAD, five-fold
patient-disjoint on the PTB-XL folds
(\cref{tab:ecg_domainc,fig:pipeline_ecg}), reproduces
the machinery end to end. Both targets are Conclusive---sex at
$\auroc\!=\!0.90$ and age$\,\geq\!65$ at $0.84$ under the best
aggregator---and both sit in regime~(i): the fusion conditions match the
encoder to within a fold's standard deviation ($\Delta\auroc\!<\!0.01$)
while the source vocabulary alone is far weaker ($0.62$ and $0.73$), so
the raw signal saturates the target and the statement channel adds
essentially nothing on top. This is the same encoder-saturated signature
\textit{EGFR} and \textit{TP53} show in LUAD, now recovered in a
different modality family with a natively graded source vocabulary.
The two-pattern synergy analysis was run for these targets as well: the
2-additive Choquet head emits a pairwise interaction index over all
$\binom{71}{2}$ statement pairs, exactly the read-out that surfaces the
$I(\text{lepidic}\!\times\!\text{solid})\!=\!+0.032$ for KRAS in LUAD
(\cref{tab:pid_regimes}).
For sex and age no statement pair reaches an interaction that lets
$\mathrm{FC}$ improve on the additive $\mathrm{PI}$
($\mathrm{FC}-\mathrm{PI}\!=\!+0.002$ and $-0.002$ respectively;
\cref{tab:ecg_domainc}), so the
synergy channel is empty and both targets remain in regime~(i)---the
negative result the model-side signature is designed to report.
The run does not exhibit an Inconclusive electrocardiogram target---sex
and age are, as expected, strongly written into the electrical
signal---so the untranslatable end of the spectrum stays anchored by
LUAD and by the pre-registered laboratory targets, whose partition into
translatable, borderline and untranslatable bands the literature already
reports.\cite{xiao2025anyecglab} What the PTB-XL run establishes is
narrower but real: the two-channel decomposition, the four-condition
protocol and the Conclusive verdict transfer intact across a modality
family, not merely in principle.

Deployment imposes one further requirement that is independent of the
taxonomy.
Large language models are entering the clinical pathology workflow
as report-drafters and second readers, but the failure mode is
the same as in the consumer setting: confident assertions
unsupported by the underlying evidence.
The dual anti-hallucination mechanism we built into the LCHAI
explanation layer is a direct response: the fuzzy linguistic labels
constrain the \emph{vocabulary} (a 0.59 prediction cannot be
\emph{high}; it must be \emph{moderate}), the NCIt+MONDO knowledge
graph constrains the \emph{entity set} (the agent cannot invent
mutations that do not exist for LUAD), and a DeepSearch literature
layer constrains the \emph{citations} (every claim must be
traceable to PubMed or a curated ontology IRI).
Deactivation-without-deletion of contested assertions completes the
mechanism by preserving an audit trail rather than silently
rewriting the record.
We argue this is the minimum bar for AI-assisted pathology
reporting and that it should generalise beyond LUAD, while noting
that the three constraints are architectural guarantees about what
the agent \emph{can} emit and not measurements of what it does emit
in the hands of a reader.

Several constraints bound what the present study establishes.
All primary numbers reported here are 5-fold patient-stratified CV on
a single retrospective cohort, TCGA-LUAD; the one external cohort we do
score, CPTAC-LUAD (detailed below), does not reproduce them off-the-shelf.
Harmonised external validation on CPTAC-LUAD with domain adaptation,
APOLLO and the EAGLE-1 silent-trial cohort is the natural next step, and a
multi-site reader study with SOLCA-CISOL and a Swiss academic medical
centre is in preparation.
The two cross-domain instantiations of
\cref{tab:crossdomain_protocol} were stated as predictions with their
cohorts, source ontologies and expected regime assignments fixed in advance,
and both are now measured: the electrocardiogram domain carries the empirical
physiological-state result of \cref{tab:ecg_domainc}, and the glioma domain
carries the results of \cref{tab:glioma_domainb}---IDH encoder-saturated,
1p/19q recovered by fusion, and MGMT Inconclusive at full power on the
$n\!=\!577$ BraTS cohort. Both show the two-channel decomposition and the
Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict transferring intact to a new modality family.
What remains predicted is now narrow---the \emph{non-additive} form of the
1p/19q interaction at full power, and the laboratory and
structural-heart-disease targets of the electrocardiogram domain; for those
the generality of the framework rests on the structural argument and the
published per-target splits we cite, now alongside these measurements rather
than in place of them.
The MGMT result---the unconstrained encoder at chance at full power, so the
instrument declines to certify the field's best-documented untranslatable
target---is the single measurement on which we invite the framework to be
judged, because it is the one a decade of negative results makes easiest to
refute.

Two senses of generalisation should be kept apart, because they are
established by different experiments. The glioma and electrocardiogram
instantiations test \emph{structural portability}---whether the three
regimes and the falsification test recur when the modality and the
source ontology change. They do not test whether the \emph{regime
assignment of a given gene} is stable, which is a separate question:
because the atoms and the verdict are population expectations
(\cref{tab:levels_crossdomain}), an assignment is a property of the
triple (source ontology, learned encoder, cohort) rather than of the
gene, so a different cohort can move a gene across the
threshold---as the KRAS case-mix dilution already shows in-sample.
Calling an assignment near-intrinsic is therefore licensed only by
concordance across independent cohorts with overlapping confidence
intervals, which a single-cohort study cannot supply.
We therefore ran a within-domain cohort-transfer test on
CPTAC-LUAD, whose whole-slide images are on The Cancer Imaging Archive
and whose matched whole-exome/whole-genome calls are on the Genomic
Data Commons and cBioPortal (\texttt{luad\_cptac\_2020}), and which
carries all six of our target genes.\cite{gillette2020cptacluad,cptacluad_data}
CPTAC-LUAD is a genuine distribution shift rather than a resample of
TCGA---enriched for never-smokers and \textit{EGFR}-mutant,
demographically more diverse tumours\cite{gillette2020cptacluad}---and
we processed it off-the-shelf: the frozen TCGA-trained pipeline applied
to the $498$ CPTAC slides from the $106$ genotyped patients with matched
H\&E, with no stain normalisation, colour calibration or domain
adaptation, and slide scores mean-pooled to the patient exactly as
pre-registered (\cref{tab:cptac_power,tab:cptac_transfer}).

The result is a clean negative. In the genotyped-with-imaging subset only
\textit{TP53} clears the $n_+\!=\!37$ estimation floor ($n_+\!=\!55$;
\cref{tab:cptac_power}); its
CPTAC AUROC is $0.53$ ($95\%$ CI $0.42$--$0.64$;
\cref{tab:cptac_transfer}), an interval that
excludes the TCGA point estimate ($0.717$) and moves the verdict from
Conclusive to Inconclusive. The collapse is not an artefact of
patient-level pooling: a slide-level read-out ($n\!=\!498$), a max, a
top-$2$/top-$3$ mean and a single-largest-slide read-out all place
\textit{TP53} between $0.49$ and $0.57$, and across all six genes and
five aggregators no CPTAC AUROC exceeds $0.62$. The remaining five
targets are underpowered---\textit{EGFR}, at $n_+\!=\!36$ in this subset,
falls just short of the floor the full cohort had cleared---so their
near-chance values are, as stated in advance, claims about power; but the
one well-powered gene is unambiguous.

Two readings survive this. The first is genuine cohort-relativity: the
morphology-to-genotype coupling is itself local to TCGA, in which case
nothing about the gene is recoverable in CPTAC by any means and
harmonisation would be wasted effort.
The second is an uncorrected technical domain gap:
CPTAC reaches the model through a DICOM-to-pyramidal-TIFF conversion on
different scanners, and TCGA-trained models are known to encode
site-specific batch signatures that correlate with mutation prevalence,
inflating in-cohort accuracy while collapsing out of it---a bias that
stain normalisation only partly removes.\cite{howard2021site}
We tested the colour component of this directly: re-running the transfer
with Reinhard stain normalisation of every tile to a representative
TCGA-LUAD reference does not recover it---\textit{TP53} stays at $0.48$
at the patient level and at most $0.54$ across the slide, mean, max,
top-$2$/top-$3$ and largest-slide aggregators, its $95\%$ CI still
excluding the TCGA point, and no aggregator recovers the TCGA value for
any gene (\cref{tab:cptac_transfer})---so a global colour/stain cast is
not the driver, and any technical account must rest on deeper scanner- or
site-level batch signatures beyond colour (Macenko stain-matrix
normalisation remains untested).
The pattern here---the high-TCGA-AUROC genes fall the most while genes
already near the floor are roughly preserved, a uniform regression toward
chance---does not by itself separate the residual technical reading from
genuine cohort-relativity. Read off-the-shelf, the TCGA verdicts are not
portable, and that much the frozen test establishes on its own: it measures
whether the \emph{learned decision function} transfers---the \ctranspath{}
encoder, the ANORAK pattern head and the selected per-gene aggregator applied
as one predictor---and it does not.

What it cannot say is which of the two readings above holds, and they
prescribe opposite investments: the second would mean the coupling is
present and only the fitted decision function failed to survive the move,
which harmonisation can repair. They are separable by experiment, and we
ran it. Freezing
only the \emph{representation}---the \ctranspath{} embeddings and ANORAK
pattern probabilities recomputed on CPTAC tiles---and re-fitting the four
conditions with patient-disjoint cross-validation inside CPTAC recovers
\textit{TP53} to $0.698$, against the TCGA value of $0.717$; the encoder-only
condition alone reaches $0.665$ (\cref{tab:cptac_refit}).
The morphology-to-genotype coupling for \textit{TP53} is therefore present in
CPTAC at strength comparable to TCGA, and the off-the-shelf collapse to
$0.53$ is a property of the transported decision function rather than of the
representation or of the tissue. In the terms of
\cref{eq:modality_ceiling} this is a method-limited gap and not a modality
ceiling: $I(W;Y)$ is plainly not the binding constraint in CPTAC, because a
model fitted on those same slides attains most of what TCGA attains.

Two controls delimit that conclusion. The cohorts differ sharply in scanner
magnification---TCGA-LUAD is $76.4\%$ $40\times$ and CPTAC-LUAD $98.6\%$
$20\times$, so identical tiling at level~$0$ presents up to four times the
tissue area per tile---which makes physical scale the most obvious mechanical
candidate. Centre-cropping and rescaling every CPTAC tile to the field of view
of a $40\times$ TCGA tile lowers every condition (encoder-only $0.561$, best
$0.632$) and widens fold-to-fold spread substantially, so scale mismatch is
not the mechanism; the crop is itself lossy, so the arm does not argue the
other way either. And the refit is within-cohort cross-validation, so it
establishes that CPTAC slides carry the signal, not that one model serves both
cohorts. Harmonised external validation with explicit domain adaptation
remains the requirement for that, and remains outstanding.

The regime assignment, by contrast, stays unresolved, and this is where the
transfer question turns out to need more than either test can give. A regime
is the contrast across the four conditions (B2/B3/\piabmil{}/\fcmil{}), not a
single head, so one number per gene cannot express it; the frozen pipeline was
deliberately never a test of it. But the refit does not adjudicate it either.
The deciding contrast $\piabmil{}-\mathrm{B2}$ is $+0.032$ with a paired
bootstrap $95\%$ CI of $-0.041$ to $+0.102$ natively, and $+0.073$
($-0.038$ to $+0.174$) under field-of-view matching (\cref{tab:cptac_refit}).
Both span zero, so under the noise-aware rule stated in Methods the
point-estimate read of (ii-a) is withheld and CPTAC neither confirms nor
contradicts the TCGA reading of~(i). That the two arms disagree on the point
estimate and agree on the verdict is the behaviour the rule exists to produce.

This exposes a requirement the estimation floor does not capture. Clearing
$n_+\!=\!37$ says a model can be \emph{fitted}, and \textit{TP53} clears it
comfortably at $n_+\!=\!55$; resolving a contrast between two fitted
conditions is a stricter and different demand, because what must exceed
sampling error is the \emph{difference} rather than either \auroc{}. Slide
count does not relieve it, since the $498$ slides are not $498$ independent
observations---the genotype is a patient-level label and slides from one
tumour share it. Scaling the observed interval width to the observed effect
puts the cohort needed to resolve this contrast at roughly five times the
present one, on the order of $500$ genotyped patients rather than $106$. We
report that as an approximate design specification for the harmonised
multi-cohort study called for above, in preference to leaving the limitation
as a complaint.

The cohort is also small relative to foundation-scale benchmarks.
At $n=687$ slides, TCGA-LUAD is one to two orders of magnitude
smaller than EAGLE ($n=8{,}461$) and CHIEF ($n=60{,}530$).
Our numbers therefore underestimate what a foundation-scale
pattern-informed pipeline could deliver and should be read as
\emph{what is reachable from the most-used public cohort}.
The 6-pattern simplex is likewise a pre-committed hypothesis space,
which means the out-of-ontology regime is by definition unreachable
inside the ANORAK ontology.
Adding micro-environment channels (tumour-infiltrating
lymphocytes,\cite{brambilla2016til} budding,\cite{kadota2015budding}
tumour-stroma ratio\cite{zhang2015tsr}) is the principled next
step for STK11 and KEAP1, and multimodal fusion with RNA-seq is the
principled next step for KEAP1 and RBM10.

The information decomposition is used as a structural lens, not a
measured quantity. We do not estimate the atoms of \cref{eq:pid}
numerically: a $512$-dimensional source is intractable at $n=687$, and
at the prevalences available here any low-dimensional surrogate would be
too noisy to quote atoms individually. We therefore report the
decomposition through its three model-side signatures---the sign of the
Choquet interaction, the SHAP embedding/pattern split and the
single-channel ablations---rather than as point estimates.
A larger cohort is the prerequisite for turning this structural claim
into a measured one; the inverse-probability-weighting correction
required once additional channels are observed on a non-random
subset\cite{choi2025icym2i} and the instance-level latent-variable
estimator of modality impact\cite{madaan2026primo} (the principled
replacement for the plug-in L5 ablation at the per-slide level) belong
to that measured extension.
Finally, the explanation layer has not been validated against
readers.
Its two constraints are enforced by construction, but whether they
change what a pathologist concludes, and at what cost in reading
time, is an empirical question this paper does not answer; the
multi-rater study noted above is designed to settle it.

The actionable verdict from this work is therefore not that the
LCHAI predictor outperforms the published competition---it does
not, and is not expected to at this cohort size---but that
\emph{the explanation layer it offers is what enables a
pathologist to use the prediction at all}.
We argue that mutation prediction at the public-cohort scale
should be reported with per-gene Conclusive/Inconclusive flags,
that pattern-informed interpretability should be the default
rather than a research add-on, and that the gene-conditional
three-regime taxonomy we propose is a more rigorous scaffold for
cross-paper comparison than the cohort-average metric currently
in use.
Because that scaffold is grounded in a partial information decomposition
rather than a naming convention, it applies unchanged to any
cross-domain medical prediction task that pairs an expert source
ontology with a learned encoder, and it converts the perennial
question of what to build next from a leaderboard argument into an
atom-conditional one.

explanation stack, NCIt+MONDO knowledge graph, fuzzy LLM agent
and the active-learning loop that ingests pathologist
corrections---is publicly accessible at
