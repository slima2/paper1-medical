\section*{Results}

% =====================================================================
\subsection*{Cohort, pattern decomposition and per-gene AUROC}
% =====================================================================

The TCGA-LUAD cohort yielded $687$ analysable diagnostic slides from
$668$ patients after exclusion of slides without a corresponding
mutation call and quality filtering (pen-marker, blur, missing
tissue; see Methods).
Mutation prevalences spanned an order of magnitude:
\textit{TP53} $38.0\%$, \textit{EGFR} $14.1\%$, \textit{KRAS}
$28.5\%$, \textit{STK11} $9.9\%$, \textit{KEAP1} $14.3\%$ and
\textit{RBM10} $5.4\%$ (Methods, \cref{tab:mutation_prevalence}).
Each slide was tiled at the level-0 resolution into
$224 \times 224$~px patches; only patches passing the tissue-mask
filter were retained, and the top $200$ patches by encoder
embedding norm were selected for MIL aggregation.

Across all six conditions ablated against a common
\ctranspath{}\cite{wang2022ctranspath} encoder backbone
(B1~\xgb{} on the six slide-level pattern fractions and their
entropy; B2 \abmil{}-embeddings; B3
\abmil{}-patterns-only; \piabmil{} concat fusion; A one-hot
patterns; \fcmil{} 2-additive Choquet),
the per-gene AUROC under 5-fold patient-stratified cross-validation
splits into two clear tiers
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
The upper tier (B2, \piabmil{}, A, \fcmil{}) reaches mean AUROC
within $\pm 0.02$ of one another on every gene, while the lower
tier (B1, B3) lags by $0.05$--$0.20$ AUROC depending on the gene.
This indicates that \emph{(i)} the choice between pattern-only and
embedding-only is a real bottleneck, and \emph{(ii)} given a
foundation-encoder embedding, the marginal architectural choice
within the upper tier is gene-dependent rather than dominant.

\subsection*{A gene-conditional three-regime taxonomy}
% =====================================================================

\begin{figure}[ht!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{signal_tree}
  \caption{\textbf{Three-regime taxonomy of mutation-predictive signal
  on TCGA-LUAD.} For each of the six target genes, the cohort-level
  AUROC under five baselines is decomposed into three regimes:
  \emph{encoder saturation}~(i) (TP53, EGFR; the \ctranspath{} embedding

  concat fusion alone recovers the signal, is not populated in this

  occupies (\cref{tab:glioma_domainb}) and that the CPTAC refit returns

When the per-gene results in
\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec} are read alongside the
per-gene biology, three distinct regimes appear
(\cref{fig:signal_tree}).

\paragraph{Regime~(i)~--~encoder saturation: TP53 and EGFR.}
On TP53 and EGFR the embedding-only baseline B2 reaches
$\auroc = 0.718 \pm 0.053$ and $0.701 \pm 0.049$ respectively;
adding the 6-d pattern channel (\piabmil{}, A or \fcmil{}) changes
the AUROC by $\leq 0.02$ (\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
This is the predicted regime when the \ctranspath{} foundation
encoder already represents the discriminative morphology---TP53's
high-grade nuclear pleomorphism\cite{ahn2022tp53highgrade,mendoza2024tp53nuclear}
and EGFR's lepidic-leaning low-grade phenotype\cite{yoshizawa2013egfrmorph,li2025lepidicgenomics}---so
the additional 6-d categorical channel adds no information.
Both genes pass the cohort-level Conclusive threshold
(mean AUROC $\geq 0.70$).

\paragraph{Regime~(ii)~--~inter-pattern co-occurrence: KRAS.}
KRAS is the cleanest demonstration of why a non-additive aggregator
matters, and the isolating comparison is between the two aggregators
that receive \emph{the same} two channels.
Fusing the pattern channel additively \emph{hurts}: \piabmil{}
concat fusion falls to $\auroc = 0.590 \pm 0.071$, below the
embedding-only B2 at $0.607 \pm 0.078$
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
The 2-additive Choquet head recovers it to $0.609 \pm 0.059$,
${+}0.019$ over additive fusion, and exposes an explicit positive
interaction index
$I(\text{lepidic}\!\times\!\text{solid})\!=\!{+}0.032$ on the
case-study slide TCGA-55-7815 (per-slide
$P(\text{KRAS}) = 0.965$, true positive) together with a negative
interaction
$I(\text{lepidic}\!\times\!\text{micropapillary})\!=\!-0.018$
(\cref{fig:kras_lepidic_solid_proxy}).
The pattern channel therefore carries KRAS information available only
from the \emph{joint} state of a pattern pair---which is precisely
what an additive head cannot express, since concat fusion sums the two
contributions where the Choquet operator can pay for their
conjunction.
The pair it recovers is the histological proxy for invasive mucinous
adenocarcinoma (IMA), the KRAS-enriched subtype historically
characterised as lepidic mucinous-acinar-solid coexistence with a
mucinous component.\cite{shim2015krasmucinous,rekhtman2013krassolid,cha2017imapatterns,hwang2017nonlepidicima}
KRAS therefore fails the cohort-level Conclusive threshold
(mean AUROC $0.609 < 0.70$) but at the per-slide level the
prediction is well-calibrated and the explanation is
clinically argumentative.
The two facts are not in tension once the level is stated
(\cref{tab:levels_of_analysis}): the cohort mean is an average over
all case pairs, whereas the IMA morphology that the interaction
index detects is present in only a subset of KRAS-mutant slides, so
a real slide-level signal is diluted into a mediocre population
statistic.
KRAS is thus Inconclusive but \emph{not} untranslatable, and it is
the clearest reason in this cohort to keep the two words apart.

\paragraph{Regime~(iii)~--~out-of-ontology: STK11 and KEAP1, with
RBM10 at the estimation floor.}
For these three genes the pattern channel yields nothing usable at
this cohort size, but for three different reasons, and only the
first two are out-of-ontology in the strict sense.

\emph{Label-space mismatch} (STK11).
The B2 embedding-only ceiling reaches
$\auroc = 0.684 \pm 0.042$ and \piabmil{} reaches
$\auroc = 0.695 \pm 0.023$, the best per-gene result for STK11
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
However, STK11's predictive signal is principally an
\emph{immune-cold tumour microenvironment}---reduced lymphocytic
infiltration, increased neutrophilic
density\cite{skoulidis2018stk11pd1,pineda2025stk11morphology}---that
the 6-pattern ANORAK ontology, designed for tumour-cell growth
patterns, does not encode.
The marginal $+0.011$ AUROC gain of pattern fusion over B2
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}) is real but
small because the signal is in the wrong label space, not in
its categorical structure.

\emph{No morphological projection} (KEAP1).
\piabmil{} reaches $\auroc = 0.610 \pm 0.071$, the best for KEAP1,
and \fcmil{} delivers $0.589$ (\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
KEAP1's mutation acts through the NRF2-mediated oxidative-stress
axis,\cite{li2011keap1papillary} a biochemical state that produces
no consistent morphological projection at the H\&E level---the
slide is asked a question (\emph{is this cell's antioxidant axis
deranged?}) that the staining method does not answer.

\emph{Collapse-to-prior} (RBM10).
\fcmil{} reaches $\auroc = 0.661 \pm 0.063$, the best for RBM10
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
With only $5.4\%$ prevalence ($n_+ = 37$ slides on the full
cohort), the discriminative signal struggles to overcome the
class prior; the model collapses much of its probability mass
to the negative class.\cite{bowman2016posteriorcollapse}
RBM10's biology---defective alternative
splicing\cite{bechara2013rbm10,cao2024rbm10egfr}---has no
established morphological correlate at the level the encoder sees.
It is nevertheless the one gene of the three whose pattern channel is
not empty, which is why the decomposition below reclassifies it as a
data-floor rather than an out-of-ontology failure.

The three-regime taxonomy is summarised in
\cref{fig:signal_tree} and informs the Conclusive/Inconclusive
verdict that \lchai{} surfaces in the clinical interface.

\subsection*{The three regimes correspond to the partial information
decomposition of the source}
% =====================================================================

The three regimes were introduced above as an empirical grouping,
but they are not free-standing: they are the three ways in which
the partial information decomposition of
\cref{eq:pid}\cite{williams2010pid,bertschinger2014unique} can be
dominated when the two sources are the pattern channel
$\mathbf{P}$ and the embedding channel $\mathbf{E}$---pattern-side
information that is \emph{redundant} with the embedding,
\emph{synergistic} with it, or \emph{absent} altogether,
respectively (\cref{tab:pid_regimes}).

\begin{table}[ht!]
\centering
\input{tables/tab_pid_regimes}
\caption{The three regimes as partial-information-decomposition
atoms.
Each row states the dominant atom of \cref{eq:pid} for one gene and
the model-side signature that evidences it; all signature values
are read off \cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec} and the L3--L5
readouts, and no additional experiment is introduced.}
\label{tab:pid_regimes}
\end{table}

This reading is falsifiable, and the falsifying test is already in
\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}.
If the KRAS advantage of \fcmil{} were a generic benefit of the
Choquet head rather than the recovery of a synergistic atom, the
non-additive aggregator would beat the additive one across the
board.
It does not.
\fcmil{} exceeds \piabmil{} on exactly two of the six genes---KRAS
($0.609$ vs $0.590$, ${+}0.019$) and RBM10 ($0.661$ vs $0.640$,
${+}0.021$)---and loses on the remaining four, including by
$0.037$ on STK11.
On the two genes of regime~(i) the two aggregators agree to within
$0.010$, which is what redundancy predicts: when the pattern
channel carries nothing the embedding does not already carry,
the choice of how to combine them cannot matter.

The decomposition also sharpens regime~(iii) in a way the
cohort-mean metric cannot.
KEAP1 and RBM10 are grouped together by their AUROC, but their
atoms differ.
For KEAP1 the pattern side is genuinely empty: the L3 split
assigns $87\%$ of the slide-level contribution to the embedding and
$13\%$ to the pattern channel, the L5 ablation costs $-44.7$\,pp of
fold-level \auroc{} when the embedding is zeroed against $-0.5$\,pp
when the pattern is zeroed (\cref{tab:pid_regimes}), and the
pattern-only B3 ($0.588$) sits below the
embedding-only B2 ($0.597$; \cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
For RBM10 the opposite holds, and it is the only gene in the cohort
for which it holds: the pattern-only B3 reaches $0.653$ and
\emph{exceeds} the embedding-only B2 at $0.642$, and the
non-additive aggregator adds a further ${+}0.021$
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}).
The pattern-side atoms for RBM10 are therefore not empty---they are
unestimable, at $n_+ = 37$ and a per-fold SD of $\pm 0.081$ on B3,
roughly four times the ${+}0.021$ non-additive gain it would have
to resolve.
This distinction is invisible in an AUROC table and it inverts the
recommended next experiment: KEAP1 needs a different source
modality (RNA-seq, NRF2 immunostaining), whereas RBM10 needs only
more slides of the same kind.

Adding KRAS completes the picture, and it is the case that forces
the level distinction of \cref{tab:levels_of_analysis} into the
open.
Four of our six genes are Inconclusive at the population level, and
in this cohort that single label covers three different states of the
world.
For KEAP1 the pattern-side atoms are empty and the verdict is
untranslatability in the strict sense.
For RBM10 they are non-empty but unestimable at $n_+ = 37$.
For KRAS they are non-empty \emph{and} estimable but carried by a
subpopulation---the IMA-associated lepidic$\times$solid morphology of
regime~(ii) above---so the cohort average over all case pairs is
diluted towards chance even where the translation holds cleanly.
The three states share a mediocre AUROC and call for a new source
modality, a larger cohort and a stratified or
conditional readout respectively.
A fourth state---a gene-level label that unions variants with different
morphological correlates---is compatible with the same reading but is
not separable at these positive-set sizes; we flag it in
\cref{tab:levels_of_analysis} as a confound on regime~(iii) rather than
adjudicating it here.
Reporting the verdict without the state, as a cohort-average metric
does, therefore does not merely lose information; on KRAS it points
to the wrong experiment, because it recommends abandoning a
modality that is in fact informative on the cases that matter.

We report this decomposition as a structural claim evidenced by its
three model-side signatures rather than as a table of numeric atoms.
A numeric atom would have to be built from the per-fold outputs of the
single-channel models B2 and B3, and those outputs move by $\pm 0.031$
to $\pm 0.081$ from fold to fold
(\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}), so any estimate derived from
them inherits at least that much sampling noise---while the quantity it
would have to resolve, whether a pattern-side atom is ${\sim}{+}0.02$
or zero, is several times smaller, as the RBM10 case above makes
explicit. Quoting a point estimate at that noise-to-signal ratio would
reproduce exactly the false precision that the
Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict exists to prevent.
What does survive the noise is the \emph{sign} of each contrast: which
condition beats which, and whether $I_{jk}$ is positive. That is what
the three signatures read and what the regime assignment turns on.
Methods states this bound and the signatures we use in place of point
estimates.

\subsection*{Six-level interpretability and dual anti-hallucination}
% =====================================================================

\lchai{} exposes six interpretability layers per slide per gene,
all derived from the same forward pass through the MIL aggregator
(\cref{fig:lchai_six_levels}):
\textbf{L1}~MIL attention overlay, indicating \emph{where} the
aggregator weighed evidence;
\textbf{L2}~pattern overlay derived from the fuzzy
membership vector $\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^5$, indicating
\emph{what} morphology the encoder saw at each high-attention
tile;
\textbf{L3}~SHAP decomposition,\cite{lundberg2017shap}
splitting the slide-level prediction into the contribution of
the $512$-d \ctranspath{} embedding and the $6$-d pattern channel;
\textbf{L4}~Choquet--Shapley values (intrinsic contribution of
each pattern) and pairwise interaction indices (\emph{e.g.} the
$+0.032$ KRAS lepidic$\,\times\,$solid of
\cref{tab:pid_regimes});
\textbf{L5}~channel ablation, recomputing the prediction with each
input channel zeroed in turn, read out per slide as
$\Delta P(\mathrm{mut})$ and per gene as the fold-level
$\Delta\auroc$;
\textbf{L6}~fuzzy linguistic labels that re-render every continuous
score as a five-step linguistic variable
(\emph{very low / low / moderate / high / very high}) with
trapezoidal membership functions whose breakpoints are fixed a
priori and held constant across every gene, slide and fold.

Levels L3--L5 are not three independent attributions but the
per-slide instruments of the same decomposition: L3 separates the
contribution shared by both channels from that of the embedding
alone, L4 reads out pairwise synergy through the $I_{jk}$, and L5
estimates each channel's unique contribution by ablation.
The stack therefore answers, for one slide, the same question the
taxonomy answers for the cohort---which atom, if any, the
morphology supplies---which is what allows a per-slide explanation
to remain informative on a gene whose cohort verdict is
Inconclusive.

The explanation layer wraps the six-level evidence bundle in an
LLM agent grounded in a three-layer knowledge graph built from
NCIt\cite{sioutos2007ncit} and MONDO.\cite{mondo_shefchek2020}
The fuzzy labels constrain \emph{how} the agent speaks and the
knowledge graph constrains \emph{what} entities and edges may appear in
the narrative.
Both constraints are architectural, enforced by the prompt
grammar and the knowledge-graph guard described in Methods, and we
report no reader-study measurement of their effect.
A live-system view of the complete stack on the KRAS case is shown in
\cref{fig:lchai_ui_stack}.

\cref{tab:literature_comparison_resultssec} places our results
beside the published competition.
On TP53 our $0.717$ sits below Coudray's $0.760$.
On EGFR our pattern-informed $0.694$ sits just under the
encoder-only ceiling ($0.701$, the value that places EGFR in the
Conclusive, encoder-saturated regime~(i)) and below Saldanha's $0.76$
(external CPTAC validation), Coudray's $0.826$ and Campanella's EAGLE
$0.890$; the last of these uses a proprietary cohort an order of magnitude
larger and a foundation model fine-tuned end-to-end for EGFR.
On STK11 ($0.695$) and KEAP1 ($0.610$) the only prior per-gene values
we are aware of are Coudray's $0.856$ and $0.675$; both exceed ours,
but they are measured on a $62$-slide held-out test set with intervals
of $[0.709,0.964]$ and $[0.466,0.865]$ against our five-fold
cross-validation on $687$ slides, so the two are not like-for-like.
Every other study drops these genes, typically because of their small
positive sets, and RBM10 is to our knowledge first reported
here at the per-slide level.

The cohort gap to CHIEF ($n=60{,}530$) and EAGLE ($n=8{,}461$)
is one to two orders of magnitude.
The contribution of this paper is not to close that gap, but to
publish \emph{what the public TCGA-LUAD cohort actually
supports} for each gene---with a Conclusive/Inconclusive
verdict, a regime-typed explanation of \emph{why}, and a
six-level interpretability stack that a pathologist can argue
against.
