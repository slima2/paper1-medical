\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Histopathology-driven prediction of driver mutations in lung
adenocarcinoma (LUAD) from haematoxylin-and-eosin (H\&E) whole-slide
images has progressed rapidly under foundation-scale cohorts
($n=8{,}461$ EAGLE, $n=60{,}530$ CHIEF), but the resulting models
remain opaque to the pathologist who must trust them, and most public
benchmarks report a single cohort-average metric that obscures
\emph{which} genes a model can actually call on \emph{which} slides.
This paper makes two empirical contributions on the public TCGA-LUAD
cohort ($n=687$ slides from $668$ patients; 5-fold patient-stratified
cross-validation) for six driver mutations
(\textit{TP53, EGFR, KRAS, STK11, KEAP1, RBM10}):
\textbf{(i)~a three-regime taxonomy} that decomposes per-gene H\&E
predictability into \emph{encoder-saturation} (TP53, EGFR;
$\auroc\in[0.701,0.718]$ already at the embedding-only baseline),
\emph{inter-pattern co-occurrence} (KRAS; a 2-additive Choquet
aggregator recovers a positive interaction
$I(\text{lepidic}\!\times\!\text{solid})\!=\!{+}0.032$ matching the
invasive-mucinous-adenocarcinoma proxy), and \emph{out-of-ontology}
(STK11, KEAP1, RBM10; signal lives outside the 6-pattern simplex
in the immune microenvironment, in a biochemical state, or below the
class-prior collapse floor); and \textbf{(ii)~a six-level per-slide
interpretability stack} (MIL attention, pattern overlay, SHAP,
Choquet--Shapley pairwise interactions, channel ablation, fuzzy
linguistic labels) wrapped in a dual anti-hallucination LLM
explanation layer grounded in a curated NCIt$+$MONDO knowledge graph.
Both contributions are delivered by \lchai{}\,v2 (Lung Cancer
Histologic Analysis with Interpretable AI), a pattern-informed
MIL pipeline that decomposes each slide into the six expert-defined
LUAD growth patterns (lepidic, acinar, papillary, micropapillary,
solid, cribriform) with a fuzzy angular-margin classifier
(macro-F1\,$=\,92.31\,\%\!\pm\!2.04$ on Zenodo-ANORAK) and propagates
the 6-d pattern probabilities to a gated-attention MIL aggregator
(\piabmil{}) and a 2-additive fuzzy Choquet integral (\fcmil{}).
A per-gene Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict (mean AUROC $\geq 0.70$)
operationalises the taxonomy at the user interface, separating
cohort-level signal presence from per-slide model confidence and
gating the explanation pathway accordingly.
Framed as an imperfect \emph{translation} from tissue morphology
to genotype---with the fuzzy angular-margin classifier rendering
the source domain and the MIL aggregator acting as translator---the
taxonomy locates each gene on a faithfulness spectrum shared with
other medical cross-domain inference tasks (radiogenomics,
retinal-to-cardiovascular, and ECG-to-function mapping), where the
achievable accuracy is bounded by the physics of the source
modality rather than by the estimator.
This lens reframes interpretability from post-hoc attribution to
\emph{exhibiting the translation}, promoting per-gene
\emph{untranslatability} to a first-class, actionable result rather
than a silent low score.
These results bound what H\&E-only mutation prediction can achieve
within the WHO/ANORAK pattern ontology, identify the genes for which
multimodal fusion (gene expression, radiology, micro-environment
channels) rather than further architectural refinement is the
natural next step, and demonstrate that pattern-informed
interpretability is most valuable precisely on the cases the
predictor gets wrong.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords.}
Lung adenocarcinoma; mutation prediction; weakly supervised learning;
explainable AI; multiple-instance learning; fuzzy logic; histological
patterns; knowledge graph; TCGA-LUAD.
