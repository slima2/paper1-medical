\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Predicting driver mutations in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) directly
from haematoxylin-and-eosin (H\&E) whole-slide images could triage
molecular testing at the cost of a scan, yet current models are
opaque to the pathologist and are judged by a single cohort-average
metric that hides \emph{which} genes are actually predictable.
On the public TCGA-LUAD cohort ($n=687$ slides, $668$ patients;
5-fold patient-stratified cross-validation) we frame H\&E-based
mutation prediction as an imperfect \emph{translation} from tissue
morphology to genotype and make two contributions.
First, a \textbf{three-regime taxonomy} of per-gene predictability:
\emph{encoder saturation} (TP53, EGFR; $\auroc\in[0.70,0.72]$ from
the embedding alone), \emph{inter-pattern co-occurrence} (KRAS; a
2-additive Choquet aggregator recovers a lepidic$\times$solid
interaction of ${+}0.032$ matching the invasive-mucinous-adenocarcinoma
phenotype), and \emph{out-of-ontology} (STK11, KEAP1, RBM10; signal
lying outside the six growth patterns---in the immune
microenvironment, a biochemical state, or below the data floor).
Second, a \textbf{six-level per-slide interpretability stack} that
recasts explanation as \emph{exhibiting the translation} and reports
per-gene \emph{untranslatability} as a first-class result, wrapped in
a knowledge-graph-grounded anti-hallucination language layer; a
per-gene Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict operationalises the taxonomy.
Delivered by \lchai{}\,v2 (source patterns rendered by a fuzzy
angular-margin classifier, macro-F1\,$=\,92.3\%$), and situated on a
fidelity spectrum shared with radiogenomics, retinal-to-cardiovascular
and ECG-to-function mapping, our results bound what H\&E alone can
achieve, show where multimodal fusion---rather than architectural
refinement---is the needed next step, and demonstrate that
pattern-informed interpretability is most valuable precisely when the
predictor is wrong.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords.}
Lung adenocarcinoma; mutation prediction; weakly supervised learning;
explainable AI; multiple-instance learning; fuzzy logic; histological
patterns; knowledge graph; TCGA-LUAD.
