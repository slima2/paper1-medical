\section*{Methods}

% =====================================================================
\subsection*{Cohort and labels}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{TCGA-LUAD acquisition.}
% OPTIONAL BEFORE SUBMISSION: if the number of patients behind the
% 1,054 downloaded WSIs is available from the GDC manifest, state it
% here. It must be >= the 668 patients that survive filtering.
We obtained $1{,}054$ FFPE-fixed haematoxylin-and-eosin (H\&E) WSIs
from the LUAD collection of The Cancer Genome Atlas via the
GDC portal.\cite{tcga2014luad,gdc2024maf}
Diagnostic slides only (DX1--DX2) were retained; frozen, snap-frozen,
and resections without a paired primary diagnostic block were
excluded.
After quality filtering (Otsu tissue mask, pen-marker detector,
blur threshold; see \emph{Slide quality control} below) and after
exclusion of slides whose patient had no MAF mutation call for any
of the six target genes, $687$ analysable slides from $668$
patients remained.
Among them, $19$ patients had $\geq 2$ analysable slides; the
cross-validation was performed at the \emph{patient} level
(patient-stratified) to prevent leakage.

\paragraph{Target genes.}
Six driver mutations were retained on the basis of
LUAD-relevance and prevalence $\geq 5\%$ in the cohort
(\cref{tab:mutation_prevalence}):
\textit{TP53} (38.0\%), \textit{EGFR} (14.1\%), \textit{KRAS}
(28.5\%), \textit{STK11} (9.9\%), \textit{KEAP1} (14.3\%) and
\textit{RBM10} (5.4\%).

The corresponding mutation calls (MAF files) were obtained from
GDC and only non-silent variants retained: missense, nonsense,
frameshift, splice-site, in-frame insertion/deletion.
Synonymous and intronic variants were excluded.

\paragraph{Slide quality control.}
Each WSI was checked for: (i)~presence of a tissue area
$\geq 20\,\text{mm}^{2}$; (ii)~absence of large pen-marker
regions ($>10\%$ of the tissue area, detected by an HSV
band-pass); (iii)~per-tile blur (variance of the Laplacian)
above an empirically fixed threshold; (iv)~absence of large
histology artefacts (tears, folds, air bubbles).
Slides failing any criterion were excluded.

% =====================================================================
\subsection*{Tile extraction and tissue mask}
% =====================================================================

Each WSI was tiled at level~0 into non-overlapping
$224 \times 224$~px patches at $0.5~\mu\text{m}/\text{px}$
(approx.\ $20\times$).
A per-slide Otsu tissue mask discarded blank or background tiles
($<10\%$ tissue).
Per-tile pen-marker, blur and artefact checks were re-applied at
the tile level.
A 4-channel input ($\text{RGB}\,+\,\text{tissue-mask}$) was passed
to the encoder.\cite{wang2022ctranspath}

% =====================================================================
\subsection*{Pattern classifier (Artefact 1)}
% =====================================================================

The six-pattern classifier was trained on the Zenodo-ANORAK
dataset\cite{anorak2024,ANORAKZenodo}---$732$ expert-annotated
tiles spanning lepidic, acinar, papillary, micropapillary,
solid and cribriform, reduced to $637$ after exclusion of
``other tissue'' (class~0) tiles---using a \ctranspath{}
backbone\cite{wang2022ctranspath} and a fuzzy angular-margin head
(FuzzyArcLoss V2; full derivation and loss-family ablation
in\cite{limareina2026fuzzyarc}).
Per-sample fuzzy modulation of the ArcFace
margin\cite{deng2019arcface} is calibrated by Optuna
($s^{*}=46.1$, $m^{*}=0.518$, $\tau^{*}=0.448$,
$50$~trials, TPE sampler, MedianPruner).
Training protocol: AdamW (lr~$1\times10^{-4}$), cosine schedule
to $10^{-6}$, $5$-epoch warm-up, $120$ epochs, batch size $32$,
fp32; MixUp disabled.
Reported macro-F1 = $92.31\% \pm 2.04$ across 15 runs (5-fold $\times$
3 seeds) with $p = 0.001$ vs SphereFace and Cohen's $d = 1.00$.
Tile probabilities $\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^5$ become the pattern
channel used downstream.

% =====================================================================
\subsection*{MIL aggregation: PI-ABMIL and FC-MIL}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Tile representation.}
For each slide we retained the top $200$ tiles by encoder
embedding norm to keep the bag size constant.
Each tile contributed a $512$-d \ctranspath{} embedding
$\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$ and a $6$-d pattern probability
$\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^5$.
Six conditions were ablated as inputs:
B1 ($7$-d slide-level pattern fractions $\,+\,$ entropy
to XGBoost\cite{chen2016xgboost}); B2 ($512$-d embeddings only to
gated ABMIL\cite{ilse2018attention}); B3 ($6$-d pattern
probabilities only); PI-ABMIL ($512+6$ concat); A (one-hot of
$\arg\max\mathbf{p}$); FC-MIL ($512$-d embeddings to gated
attention $+$ $6$-d patterns to a fuzzy Choquet head).

\paragraph{PI-ABMIL.}
Gated attention\cite{ilse2018attention} computes
$\alpha_i = \softmax(\mathbf{v}^\top \tanh(\mathbf{W}_a
[\mathbf{e}_i;\mathbf{p}_i]) \odot \sigmoid(\mathbf{W}_b
[\mathbf{e}_i;\mathbf{p}_i]))$, and the slide representation is
$\mathbf{z} = \sum_i \alpha_i [\mathbf{e}_i;\mathbf{p}_i]$
followed by a linear gene head.
Hidden dim $128$, two-layer attention; $\approx 199{,}553$
parameters total.

\paragraph{FC-MIL (2-additive fuzzy Choquet).}
The slide-level pattern vector $\mathbf{P}\in\Delta^5$ is
aggregated by a differentiable
$2$-additive Choquet
integral~\cite{grabisch1996application,grabisch1997kapprox}.
A general fuzzy measure over $n\!=\!6$ patterns would require
$2^{n}\!-\!2\!=\!62$ parameters; the 2-additive approximation
reduces this to $6 + \binom{6}{2}\!=\!21$ parameters, fully
determined by singletons and pairs:
\begin{equation}
g(A) = \sum_{k\in A}\phi_k \;+\; \sum_{\{j,k\}\subseteq A} I_{jk},
\qquad A \subseteq \{1,\ldots,6\},
\label{eq:fuzzy_measure}
\end{equation}
where the singletons $\phi_k$ are Shapley
values~\cite{shapley1953value} (intrinsic contribution of pattern
$k$) and $I_{jk}$ are pairwise interaction indices
(positive $\Rightarrow$ synergy, negative $\Rightarrow$ redundancy).
We parameterise the singletons via a softmax
$\phi_k = e^{\psi_k}/\sum_l e^{\psi_l}$ on a learnable vector
$\psi\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$; the interactions are stored as a learnable
upper-triangular matrix with an $L_1$ sparsity prior
$\mathcal{R}_\text{int}=\lambda_I\sum_{j<k}|I_{jk}|$ and a soft
monotonicity penalty
$\mathcal{R}_\text{mono} =
\sum_{A\subseteq B}\max(0,\,g(A)\!-\!g(B))$
sampled by Monte Carlo at every batch.

For a slide-level fuzzy pattern probability vector
$\mathbf{P}\in\Delta^{5}$ sorted in ascending order
$P_{(1)}\!\leq\!P_{(2)}\!\leq\!\cdots\!\leq\!P_{(6)}$, the discrete
Choquet integral is
\begin{equation}
C_g(\mathbf{P}) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{6}
\bigl[P_{(k)}-P_{(k-1)}\bigr]\, g\!\left(A_{(k)}\right),
\quad A_{(k)} \!=\! \{(k),\ldots,(6)\},
\label{eq:choquet_integral}
\end{equation}
with $P_{(0)}\!:=\!0$.
The output $\mathbf{c}_s\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$ is concatenated with the
gated-attention embedding $\mathbf{z}_\mathrm{attn}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$
and projected to the per-gene prediction.
\fcmil{} is the architectural primitive that exposes
pattern--pattern interactions as the $15$ scalars $I_{jk}$ for
direct clinical inspection at inference time.

\subsection*{Partial information decomposition of the pattern and
embedding channels}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Decomposition.}
The three-regime taxonomy of C1 is stated as a claim about how the
information the slide carries about the mutation label is
distributed between the two channels the aggregator receives.
Let $Y\in\{0,1\}$ be the per-gene mutation label, and let the two
sources be the slide-level pattern channel $\mathbf{P}\in\Delta^{5}$
and the \ctranspath{} embedding channel
$\mathbf{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$.
Partial information decomposition
(\pid{})\cite{williams2010pid,bertschinger2014unique} splits the
joint mutual information\cite{cover2006elements} into four
non-negative atoms,
\begin{equation}
I\!\left(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{E};Y\right) \;=\;
\Ired \;+\; \Iuni{\mathbf{P}} \;+\; \Iuni{\mathbf{E}} \;+\; \Isyn ,
\label{eq:pid}
\end{equation}
where $\Ired$ is the information available \emph{redundantly} from
either channel, $\Iuni{\mathbf{P}}$ and $\Iuni{\mathbf{E}}$ the
information available from one channel only, and $\Isyn$ the
information available only from their \emph{joint} state.
Since the decomposition is not unique, we adopt the
Bertschinger~et~al.\ definition of these atoms\cite{bertschinger2014unique}
as the reference decomposition against which our model-side signatures are
read.
\Cref{eq:pid} makes the three regimes mutually exclusive
predictions rather than post-hoc descriptions: regime~(i) is
$\Ired$-dominated (so any fusion of $\mathbf{P}$ into $\mathbf{E}$
is a no-op); regime~(ii)---inter-pattern co-occurrence---is the
regime in which the pattern channel carries information the encoder
does not, and it admits two sub-forms: \emph{additive
complementarity} (ii-a), $\Iuni{\mathbf{P}}>0$ with
$\Isyn\!\approx\!0$, in which concat fusion already recovers the
signal ($\mathrm{PI}\!>\!\max(\mathrm{B2},\mathrm{B3})$ while
$\mathrm{FC}\!\approx\!\mathrm{PI}$), and \emph{non-additive synergy}
(ii-b), $\Isyn>0$, which only a non-additive aggregator can realise
($\mathrm{FC}\!>\!\mathrm{PI}$ with $\max_{j<k} I_{jk}>0$); and
regime~(iii) requires
$\Iuni{\mathbf{P}}\!\approx\!\Isyn\!\approx\!0$ with
$I(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{E};Y)$ itself near the estimation floor.

\paragraph{Correspondence with the Choquet interaction index.}
For a $2$-additive fuzzy measure, \cref{eq:fuzzy_measure} assigns
the pair $\{j,k\}$ a value that exceeds the sum of its singletons
exactly when $I_{jk}>0$; the aggregator therefore pays for the
\emph{joint} activation of patterns $j$ and $k$ over and above
their separate activations.\cite{grabisch1996application,marichal2000determination}
This is the decision-theoretic counterpart of a positive
synergistic atom in \cref{eq:pid}, and, symmetrically, $I_{jk}<0$
is the counterpart of redundancy between the two patterns.
We therefore treat the learned $I_{jk}$ as the model-side estimator
of pattern-pair synergy, and the additive concat fusion of
\piabmil{} as an aggregator whose hypothesis space forces
$\Isyn$ to be discarded.
The three remaining interpretability levels estimate the remaining
atoms: the L3 SHAP embedding/pattern split tracks $\Ired$ versus
$\Iuni{\mathbf{E}}$, L4 reads out $\Isyn$ through the $I_{jk}$, and
the L5 channel ablation is the plug-in estimator of the unique atom
of the ablated channel.

\paragraph{Reading the atoms from the model-side signature.}
We do not estimate the atoms of \cref{eq:pid} numerically; a direct
estimate on the $512$-d channel is intractable at $n=687$. Instead each
atom's \emph{presence} is read from the model-side signatures introduced
above---the sign of the learned Choquet interaction $I_{jk}$ for
$\Isyn$ (L4), the SHAP embedding/pattern split for $\Ired$ versus
$\Iuni{\mathbf{E}}$ (L3), and the single-channel ablations for the
unique atoms (L5)---together with the contrast among the four training
conditions B2/B3/PI/FC (\cref{tab:atom_operationalization}). The regime is
therefore assigned from the
\emph{direction} of these signatures, never from a point estimate of an
atom or from the magnitude of any \auroc{}: regime~(ii) is declared
when fusion adds to the encoder
($\mathrm{PI}\!>\!\max(\mathrm{B2},\mathrm{B3})$)---its non-additive
sub-form (ii-b) additionally requiring $\mathrm{FC}\!>\!\mathrm{PI}$
with $\max_{j<k} I_{jk}>0$, and its additive sub-form (ii-a) holding
when $\mathrm{FC}\!\approx\!\mathrm{PI}$; regime~(i) when fusion does
not add to the encoder and $I_{jk}\!\le\!0$; and regime~(iii) when the
pattern channel is uninformative under every condition, with the total
signal near the estimation floor. This keeps the regime axis orthogonal
to the Conclusive/Inconclusive verdict (\cref{eq:conclusive_verdict}),
the sole magnitude test---so KRAS is regime~(ii-b) via
$I(\text{lepidic}\!\times\!\text{solid})\!=\!+0.032$ and
$\mathrm{FC}\!>\!\mathrm{PI}$ yet Inconclusive at a cohort mean of
$0.61$ (\cref{tab:pid_regimes}).
Because the assignment turns on the \emph{sign} of a contrast rather than
on an absolute \auroc{}, it transfers across cohorts and modalities without
recalibrating any per-cohort threshold; a taxonomy read off absolute
\auroc{} would instead be a property of the cohort it was fitted on and would
not travel.

\paragraph{Scope and corrections.}
Three methodological refinements from the recent literature could in
principle bear on our decomposition; we state for each whether it is
needed here.
The first concerns missing channels. When the two sources are not both
observed on every case, an information estimate is biased and must be
reweighted by the inverse probability that a channel was
observed.\cite{choi2025icym2i} That correction is unnecessary here
because the pattern channel $\mathbf{P}$ and the embedding channel
$\mathbf{E}$ are both computed from the \emph{same} H\&E slide, so every
case carries both. It becomes mandatory only for the multimodal
extension proposed in the Discussion, where the additional channels
(RNA-seq, micro-environment quantification) exist for a
non-randomly-selected subset of TCGA-LUAD; we therefore specify it as
part of that future protocol rather than applying it retrospectively.
The second concerns how we measure a channel's per-slide contribution.
We use the plug-in L5 ablation---zeroing a channel and reading the drop
in \auroc{}---whereas the principled replacement is a latent-variable
estimator of instance-level modality impact,\cite{madaan2026primo} which
we adopt in the external-validation protocol.
The third is the reason we never quote a numerical value for an
individual atom of \cref{eq:pid}. Any such estimate would have to be
built from the outputs of the two single-channel models
(B2, encoder only; B3, pattern only), and those models are themselves
noisy: their per-fold \auroc{} varies by $\pm 0.031$ to $\pm 0.081$
across folds (\cref{tab:mutation_luad_resultssec}). Because the
differences between the atoms we would be trying to separate are no
larger than that fold-to-fold noise, a point estimate of any single atom
would be dominated by sampling error. We therefore report the
decomposition per gene as a \emph{structural} claim---which atoms are
present, and with what sign---evidenced by its three model-side
signatures (\cref{tab:pid_regimes}), and do not attempt point estimates
of individual atoms.

\subsection*{Cross-validation and statistical analysis}
% =====================================================================

Five-fold patient-stratified cross-validation; folds were
created with mutation-stratified shuffling at the patient level to
preserve gene prevalence.
Per-fold metrics: \auroc{} (primary, prevalence-invariant),
\auprc{}\cite{davis2006auprc} (secondary, prevalence-sensitive),
positive class operating thresholds via Youden's
$J$.\cite{youden1950index}

\paragraph{Conclusive verdict criterion.}
The cohort-level Conclusive/Inconclusive flag
(\cref{tab:viz_folds_methods}) is defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Conclusive}(\mathrm{gene}) \iff
\frac{1}{5}\sum_{f=1}^{5}\auroc_{f}^{(\mathrm{gene})} \;\geq\; 0.70,
\label{eq:conclusive_verdict}
\end{equation}
where $\auroc_f^{(\mathrm{gene})}$ is the held-out AUROC of fold $f$
under the best MIL aggregator for that gene.
This is a population-level signal-presence flag, \emph{not} a
per-slide confidence threshold; the per-slide $P$-value (the
softmax-normalised mutation probability of the gene-specific head)
is reported independently and inherits the calibration of the
underlying \piabmil{} or \fcmil{} classifier.

\subsection*{Levels of analysis and the scope of a verdict}
% =====================================================================

Every quantity in this paper is estimated at one of four levels, and
the claims each level supports are different
(\cref{tab:levels_of_analysis}).
We state the correspondence explicitly because the central verdict of
the framework is defined at one level and is routinely read as though
it held at another.

\paragraph{The verdict is a population statement.}
\Cref{eq:conclusive_verdict} thresholds a mean \auroc{}.
The \auroc{} is the probability that a randomly drawn positive case
is ranked above a randomly drawn negative one; it is a property of
the \emph{joint distribution of case pairs} in the cohort and it
contains no information about the posterior assigned to any named
slide.
Two inferences are therefore invalid, and we avoid both throughout.
The \emph{upward} fallacy reads a Conclusive verdict as a licence to
present every per-slide call as confident; the cohort mean is an
average and says nothing about the tails.
The \emph{downward} fallacy---the one that matters more here---reads
an Inconclusive verdict as evidence that the source modality carries
no information about the target.
It does not follow, because the cohort mean is taken over a
population that may be morphologically heterogeneous with respect to
the target.

\paragraph{An Inconclusive verdict is compatible with three distinct
states.}
Writing $\ell$ for the level at which a claim is made, a gene may
fail \cref{eq:conclusive_verdict} because
\emph{(a)}~the source-side atoms of \cref{eq:pid} are empty at every
$\ell$, which is untranslatability in the strict sense;
\emph{(b)}~those atoms are non-empty on a subpopulation of cases
defined by source-domain morphology, so the signal is real at the
slide level but is diluted in an average taken over the whole
cohort; or
\emph{(c)}~the atoms are non-empty but unestimable at the available
positive-set size.
These have the same cohort-mean signature and opposite remedies---a
different source modality, a stratified or conditional readout, and
more slides respectively---so we report which of the three we believe
holds, with the model-side evidence for it, rather than reporting the
verdict alone.
KRAS is our instance of \emph{(b)} and is discussed in Results; it is
also the reason the six-level stack is evaluated per slide rather
than summarised into a cohort statistic.

\paragraph{Consequence for the interpretability stack.}
Levels L1--L6 are computed at the slide and tile levels and are never
aggregated into the verdict, and the verdict is never propagated into
the linguistic layer as a per-case confidence statement.
The LLM agent receives both, together with the level at which each
was estimated, and is constrained (\cref{eq:trap_mf} and the
knowledge-graph guard below) from asserting a cohort property of a
single case or a case property of the cohort.

\subsection*{Six-level interpretability stack}
% =====================================================================

\textbf{(L1) ABMIL attention overlay:} the $\alpha_i$ weights are
rendered as a heat-map on the slide thumbnail.
\textbf{(L2) Pattern overlay:} per-tile $\arg\max\mathbf{p}$
rendered as a discrete colour layer; each tile is hover-clickable.
\textbf{(L3) SHAP:}
TreeSHAP\cite{lundberg2017shap,lundberg2020treeshap} for B1;
DeepSHAP for the MIL conditions; the slide-level SHAP value is
decomposed into the embedding-channel and pattern-channel
contributions.
\textbf{(L4) Choquet--Shapley and pairwise
interactions:}\cite{shapley1953value,grabisch1996application}
the FC-MIL parameter set is read out per slide.
\textbf{(L5) Channel ablation:} the prediction is recomputed with
each input channel zeroed in turn.
The per-slide readout is the shift in that slide's posterior,
$\Delta P(\mathrm{mut})$; its cohort counterpart, reported per gene
in \cref{tab:pid_regimes}, is the $\Delta\auroc$ of the held-out
fold. Only the latter is a population quantity, and the two are
never interchanged (\cref{tab:levels_of_analysis}).
\textbf{(L6) Fuzzy linguistic labels:} continuous scores
$x\!\in\![0,1]$ (per-slide $P$-value, Shapley values
$\phi_k$, interaction magnitudes $|I_{jk}|$) are mapped to one of
five linguistic categories
$\{\mathrm{VL},\mathrm{L},\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H},\mathrm{VH}\}$ via
the trapezoidal membership functions
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{cat}}(x;\,a,b,c,d) =
\max\!\left(0,\;
\min\!\left(1,\,\frac{x-a}{b-a},\,\frac{d-x}{d-c}\right)\right),
\label{eq:trap_mf}
\end{equation}
The breakpoints are a uniform five-band partition of $[0,1]$
($\mathrm{VL}\!:\![0,0.10]$, $\mathrm{L}\!:\![0.10,0.30]$,
$\mathrm{M}\!:\![0.30,0.70]$, $\mathrm{H}\!:\![0.70,0.90]$,
$\mathrm{VH}\!:\![0.90,1.00]$) with a symmetric overlap of $0.10$
between adjacent trapezoids.
They are a reporting convention rather than a fitted parameter:
they are fixed before any result is computed and held constant
across every gene, slide and fold, so no calibration data enter the
linguistic layer and the layer adds no researcher degree of freedom
to the reported numbers.
The LLM agent below is permitted to use only these categorical
words when stating evidence strength, removing spurious decimal
precision and monotonic claims on probabilities inside the
\emph{Moderate} band.

The mapping of L3--L5 onto the atoms of \cref{eq:pid} is given
above; it is why the stack is reported as a single artefact rather
than as a menu of independent overlays.

% =====================================================================
\subsection*{LLM explanation layer with dual anti-hallucination}
% =====================================================================

Each slide-level evidence bundle (gene, P-value, L1--L6 outputs,
Conclusive flag, similar curated cases) is rendered as a structured
JSON document and passed to a prompt-engineered LLM agent
(provider-agnostic; we validated with GPT-4 and Claude Sonnet).
The agent is instructed:
\textit{(a)} to use only the fuzzy linguistic labels of
\cref{eq:trap_mf} for probabilities (constraining \emph{how} it
speaks);
\textit{(b)} to invoke only entities/edges present in the
three-layer knowledge graph $\mathcal{K} = (V, E)$ depicted in
\cref{fig:kg_three_layers}, built from
NCIt~\cite{sioutos2007ncit} (concept layer $V_C$),
MONDO~\cite{mondo_shefchek2020} (disease layer $V_D$) and a
dynamic per-case DeepSearch literature layer $V_L$ with PMID
provenance attached to every $e\in E$
(constraining \emph{what} it can claim);
\textit{(c)} to surface any deactivated edges as
``previously hypothesised, now contested'' rather than deleting
them, preserving full auditability.
